### S703 — 2026-09-08 — The billing export stops pricing Suite as a bundle, and the copy gets a guard

**No service worker bump (no app code changed). `billing-export` must be pasted into the Supabase
dashboard to take effect — it has no deploy step, which is the whole reason this drifted.**

`supabase/functions/billing-export/index.ts` is a deliberate second copy of `clientMrr.js`: it is
pasted into the dashboard editor, so it cannot import anything from the bundle. It held a
`SUITE_BUNDLES` table — starter 5,300 / growth 5,800 / pro 6,500 — and `computeBilling` returned
early on it, **ignoring IMS, HR and POS entirely**. That is the pricing model this product retired
in S552, when Crest Suite Pro became an add-on bought per outlet on top of the modules rather than a
bundle containing them. The app side moved and the copy did not.

**So the two repos disagreed outright on every Suite client.** A Growth client on all three modules
plus Suite is 8,700 in Crest and synced as 6,500 — and hss-suite bills off this payload. S701 made
every other price live and gave Suite a `plan_prices.suite` key this function did not read, which is
what surfaced it.

Suite is additive now, priced from `plan_prices.suite` with the shipped 2,000 as the fallback, and
`pricing_basis` no longer emits `'suite_bundle'`. **Checked before changing the value rather than
after**: hss-suite prints `pricing_basis` in one detail panel (`CrestClients.jsx:305`) and never
branches on it, and the column is plain `text` with no CHECK — so the string simply stops appearing.

**`next_renewal_at` was wrong for the same reason and was easy to miss.** It took Suite's date ALONE
whenever Suite was on, which is right only while Suite replaces the modules — so a Suite client whose
IMS expired next month reported the Suite date and the earlier renewal went unseen. Every active
window is a candidate now. **When a model changes, every derived field computed under the old one
changes with it**; the money was the reported symptom and the date was sitting right beside it.

**The real fix is that the copy is now asserted, not trusted.** A comment saying "mirror any change
here by hand" is not a mechanism — it is the thing that failed. `clientMrr.test.js` reads the Edge
Function's source (the technique `nepalMoney.test.js` already uses) and asserts no `SUITE_BUNDLES`,
every price read from `planPrices?.*`, and each hand-copied fallback equal to `DEFAULT_PLAN_PRICES`
— so repricing in `pricingPlans.js` fails in the suite instead of going unnoticed for months.
Verified by perturbation: changing the function's Suite fallback to 2,500 fails the assertion.
Full-line comments are stripped before matching, so the prose *about* the retired bundle does not
trip the assertions *against* it.

Filed on the hss-suite side of `docs/CROSS-REPO.md`: every Suite client's `monthly_amount` moves
upward on the next sync and `next_renewal_at` may come earlier. Nothing over there has to change to
accept the new payload.

**Then a screenshot of that screen settled it, and corrected this entry.** It was written claiming
anything already invoiced off the old figure was understated — a commercial question to go and ask.
It is not: all four synced clients sit in `Unlinked (4)`, and both `crestBillable` and Send to
Finance refuse without a `customer_id`, so no `revenue_ledger` row can exist. The understatement is
**prospective only** and there is nothing to correct. Worth naming the habit: the consequence of a
pricing bug is a question about the other system's STATE, and it is cheaper to look than to warn.

The same screen is the proof this fix was needed and the proof S701 works, in one frame. BHATTI
CHOILA and CASA ACAI CAFE both read **Rs. 6,500.00** — the retired `pro` bundle price exactly —
while Longtail e-media reads **Rs. 7,100.00**, which is `2,600 + 2,400 + 2,100`: IMS Growth plus the
admin's EDITED HR and POS prices. The function was already carrying live `plan_prices` through to
the sister app for the modules; Suite alone was frozen.

And one number on it belongs to an older row: that screen's *Crest Suite MRR* tile said **Rs.
13,000.00**, of which **6,500 is the dummy BHATTI CHOILA client** the ledger has had open since
2026-09-02. Half the platform revenue that screen reports is fictional, and it sits in the headline
KPI rather than a stray row — the row was filed as a tidy-up and reads as one until you see where
the number lands.

### S702 — 2026-09-08 — Plan Pricing shows monthly, annual and ARR at once, three modules to a row

**Service worker `crest-v239` → `crest-v240`. No migration, no Edge Function deploy.**

Rearranged from a hand-drawn layout, and every item on it was a real complaint about the screen
S701 had just built.

**Monthly and annual were two tabs, for a figure that is not a second price.** Annual is
`annualOf(monthly)` — one expression, ×0.75 — so putting it behind a toggle made a derived number
somewhere you have to go and look, and the Annual tab was a grid of read-only boxes with the Save
button disabled under it. Both are on screen together now: the input is the monthly price and the
annual rate sits under it, with the **ARR beside it** — the yearly figure is what an operator is
actually deciding with and it used to be a multiplication done in the head. Only Monthly is
editable, which is still what stops the two from drifting apart. `pricingCycle` is gone.

**HR, POS and Suite were three full-width cards holding one field each**, so a tab with four
numbers on it stood a screen and a half tall. One `form-grid-3` row now — the same class the IMS
tiers use, which collapses to a single column at 768px on its own.

**The header card's paragraph became a `Tip`.** Six lines of explanation above the fields it
explains is a paragraph nobody reads twice; the facts that matter (it is the platform's list, not
the viewed client's; it reaches the public page, Help, the module picker and every MRR figure;
annual is calculated) are on hover over the heading, with the Save button in the space the prose
had. The Save stays on this card rather than the page header for S684's reason — the page-level
button writes the viewed client's row — and now that the tab fits one screen, everything it commits
is visible with it.

**Three fields shared one accessible name.** HR, POS and Suite each labelled their input "Monthly
price", so a screen reader announced three identical controls. Each input takes an `aria-label`
naming its module and still containing the visible text, which is what WCAG 2.5.3 asks for. The
card headings also moved from `MODULE_COLORS` to `MODULE_INK` — a signal colour used as TEXT takes
the ink variant, and these had been using the fill token since before that rule existed.

**`Settings.planPricing.test.jsx`** is the first component test for this page: it asserts the tab
calls `savePlatformPlanPrices` and never `saveSettings` (S701's defect, which no unit test could
have caught), that the seed falls back per field over the live row's dead legacy keys, and that
both derived lines re-render as a price is typed.

Two traps worth recording from writing it. A `jest.mock` factory may only close over variables
whose names begin with `mock` — and the rename that fixes that will happily rename
`DEFAULT_PLAN_PRICES` too. More useful: **a context mock returning a fresh object literal per call
hangs the suite rather than failing it.** Settings reseeds local state from `settings` and
`recipeCategories` in effects keyed on those objects, so a new identity every render is an infinite
loop; in the app both are React state and stable, so only a mock can get this wrong. Hoist every
mocked object to a module constant.

Same rule as ever for the price field itself: `priceField` is a plain function returning JSX, not a
component declared inside render — a component declared there is a new type on every keystroke, so
React would unmount the input and the field would lose focus after one digit.

### S701 — 2026-09-08 — A price changed in Settings now reaches the page a buyer reads

**Service worker `crest-v238` → `crest-v239`. No migration, no Edge Function deploy.**

Reported from a screenshot: Settings → Plan Pricing said Crest HR 2,400 and Crest POS 2,100, and
`/pricing` said 2,600 and 2,000. Both were telling the truth about different things. The admin tab
writes `settings.plan_prices`; the public page, the Help page's Plan & Pricing tab and the admin's
own module picker each did `import { HR_PRICING } from '../data/pricingPlans'`. **The override
reached every internal revenue figure and nothing a customer could see** — which is the wrong half
to get right. The live platform row had carried 2,400/2,100 since the day it was typed.

**Everything that prints a price now resolves it first.** `resolvePricing(planPrices)` in
`pricingPlans.js` returns the four sellable things — the three IMS tiers, HR, POS, Suite — with the
admin's figures laid over the shipped ones. The constants are still the fallback and still hold the
feature lists; they are simply no longer what anyone renders. Two properties it needed:

- **It falls back per FIELD.** The live row has no `ims` key at all (it still carries the JSONB
  column's original DEFAULT — flat `starter`/`growth`/`pro` at 5,000/8,000/12,000, dead since IMS
  tiers moved under `ims`). An all-or-nothing fallback would have thrown away the HR and POS prices
  that ARE set, or resolved the tiers to undefined.
- **0 survives.** A module priced at 0 is a real configuration — `clientMrr.js` deliberately lists a
  zero-priced live module rather than dropping it — so the test is "is this a number", not
  `|| fallback`, which would quietly restore 2,000 over a deliberate zero.

**Annual is one expression now.** `annualOf()` (monthly × 0.75, rounded) replaces the copy inside
Settings and the published `annual` field on every constant; a test asserts each of those fields
already equalled the derivation, which is why no default-priced figure moved. Live check against
the real database, signed out: HR 2,400/mo and 1,800 annual, POS 2,100 and 1,575 — the annual
figures could only be right if they were re-derived, since the constants say 1,950 and 1,500.

**Three more surfaces were reading the wrong row, not just the wrong file.** `settings` is whichever
row the CURRENT session read, and a client session reads its own, where `plan_prices` is null. So
Admin → Clients' per-client MRR, the Admin Dashboard's platform MRR/ARR **and the Subscription
Agreement an Owner signs** all silently reverted to the shipped prices the moment a client was
selected. `SettingsContext` now reads the platform row's prices alongside the platform support
contact it already fetched, and exposes `planPrices` (the raw table) and `pricing` (resolved). One
extra column on a read that was already happening.

**The tab could also save into a row nothing reads.** Plan Pricing edited `form` and committed
through the page-level `save()`, which targets the viewed client — so an admin who had a client
selected changed the price, got "✓ Saved", and moved nothing. It saves through
`savePlatformPlanPrices()` to the `client_id`-NULL row now, with its own button beside its own
fields (S684's rule), and the page-level button no longer renders on that tab. `saveSettings()`
strips `plan_prices` from its payload for the mirror-image reason: **a column with a dedicated
writer must not also ride along in the general one**, or saving any other tab after a price change
puts the stale table back.

**Crest Suite Pro is priced in the tab now.** It was the one sellable thing with no override at
all, so the pricing page could track three of its four cards and printed a stale figure on the
fourth. It reads from `plan_prices.suite`, discounts annually like every other line, and a client
already on the default price bills exactly what it billed before — the published 2,000/1,500 pair
was already the 25% derivation.

**A price that is not known yet is a shimmer, not a default figure.** The numbers arrive with the
platform row a beat after first paint, so rendering the shipped price and swapping it when the read
lands would let a visitor read one price and act on another, on a page headlined "Simple, honest
pricing". The `.skeleton` placeholder is em-width so the swap does not move the layout.

Not touched, and worth knowing: `supabase/functions/billing-export/index.ts` keeps its own ported
copy of this arithmetic (it is pasted into the dashboard editor, so it cannot import), and its
`SUITE_BUNDLES` table still models Suite as a bundle that REPLACES the per-module sum — the pricing
model from before S552. It reads `plan_prices` for IMS/HR/POS, so those follow; Suite does not.

### S700 — 2026-09-08 — The dashboards get one vertical rhythm, and the KPI card stops being four cards

**Service worker `crest-v236` → `crest-v238`. No migration, no Edge Function deploy.**

Started from a screenshot: the weekday initials under Daily Purchases vs Sales were cut off. They
were not cramped — Recharts places tick text at `axisY + tickSize(6) + tickMargin(2)` even with
`tickLine={false}`, so in a 160px card with a 26px axis band the second line's baseline landed at
**162px inside a 160px SVG** and the SVG's own edge clipped it. Band to 34, card to 184.

That prompted a spacing analysis of all five dashboards, which found **14 distinct vertical
intervals** against a documented 4/8/16/24 scale — and four real defects.

**Group Console and Owner Dashboard butted shadowed cards edge to edge.** `.stat-grid` is `gap: 0`
because `.stat-card` collapses each pair of adjacent borders into ONE drawn line, via `-1px`
margins, with its shadow dropped to do it. GroupDashboard put `.card` in that grid and
OwnerDashboard put a hand-rolled box in it — neither carries the seam pull or the shadow reset, so
thirteen KPI cells rendered 2px double rules with their shadows overlapping at every join. Exactly
the "two depth models arguing" the class comment warns about, in the two files that had not read it.

**"Show 6 more" sat flush against the chart row.** The disclosure toggle had `marginTop: 10` and
nothing below it, and `imsChartsAndTables` opens with a row carrying `marginBottom` and no
`marginTop` — so the gap under a control was **0 collapsed and 14 expanded**. The space under a
control must not depend on what the control says.

**The three module columns did not start on the same line.** HR carried `marginTop: showIms ? 6 : 0`
and POS the same — written when the module blocks STACKED. Once S438/S439 made them grid columns
that 6px stopped being a separator and became a column OFFSET, so Inventory's heading sat 6px above
Human Resources' and Point of Sale's. On the phone the same margins compounded the other way:
14 + the grid's own 16px row-gap + 6 = **36px** between modules against 14 everywhere else.

**Sections did not read as sections.** ClientDashboard put 10px between a module heading and its
own content and 14px between one section and the next — a **1.4x** contrast. HrDashboard states the
right target in a comment and hits it: 8px label→content, 28px group→group, 3.5x. Below that ratio
the heading floats free of the block it names. `.dash-section` (28) and `.dash-row` (16) are the
pair now, both self-zeroing on `:last-child` — which is what makes them safe to apply
unconditionally, since dashboard JSX is conditional almost everywhere and which block renders last
depends on which modules a client bought. They step to 16/8 under 768px, where `.main-content`
drops 32 → 16 and a 28px group gap would separate sections by nearly twice the page's own margin.

**The Admin Dashboard shifted layout on load**: its skeleton grid was `minmax(190px)` / `gap: 14` /
`alignItems: start` against the real strip's `158` / `8` / `stretch`. A skeleton that does not match
its own content is a layout shift with extra steps.

**And the KPI card was four cards.** ClientDashboard, OwnerDashboard and AdminDashboardOverview had
each typed `.stat-card`'s five box properties out inline and then landed on their own padding —
`10px 14px`, `14px 16px`, `12px 14px` against the class's `20px`. `stat-card--compact` is the dense
tier as a real class (`8px 16px`, no seam pull, shadow kept, for a strip that has a real gap), and
`card--compact` (16px) replaces the six inline `.card` padding overrides, ChartCard's included. The
declared 8px gutters in `.stat-grid--compact` had also been rendering as **7px**, because
`.stat-card`'s seam pull is meaningless once there is space between cells.

Everything else was normalisation: 14 distinct vertical values down to 4/6/8/16/28 across the five
files, with `.btn`'s own `8px 16px` and 6px icon gap taken instead of five hand-rolled button
paddings. Two values were deliberately left alone — the adoption pills' measured `gap: 3` (S642
sized that row against the grid floor; changing it re-opens a wrap that was measured) and the
`4px 6px` / `-4px -6px` hit-area pair, whose halves must match.

The cascade was verified by measurement rather than by reading: `.stat-grid dash-section` resolves
to 28 at 1440 and 16 at 390, `.dash-row` inside a `.stat-grid--compact` resolves to 16 (the later
declaration wins the tie, which is why `.dash-section`/`.dash-row` are declared after both grids),
and both zero on `:last-child` at each width. The hover transition the three `kpiCard()`s were each
declaring inline moved onto `.interactive-card`, where the border it animates lives.
