**One migration — `20260908120000_demand_forecast_sample_count.sql`, two nullable integer

`CACHE_NAME` and `APP_VERSION` bumped to `crest-v229`. Aashish sent a screenshot of the Demand

page had 57 of them per day, 38 below 0.5. The re-analysis found the presentation was the smaller
problem: the engine had four defects and the page's promise ("for purchasing and prep planning")
had no consumer at all — the only reader of `demand_forecast_daily` was the Roster, and it reads
covers-level rows only. Four decisions were put to Aashish and he took the recommended option on

**The engine, `src/utils/demandForecastData.js`, and its arithmetic, now in
`src/utils/demandForecastMath.js`.** The split is the S693 shape: the pure module imports no
Supabase client, so its nine exports have a test file — `demandForecastMath.test.js`, 15 tests —
where the old file's three pure exports never could. What changed inside:

1. **Forecast Revenue is before VAT.** `buildDailyHistory` took `computeOrderAmounts(...).net`,
   the rounded amount payable *including* VAT, and was the only caller in the app that hard-coded
   `vatReg = true` instead of passing the client's setting — so a non-VAT-registered outlet got VAT
   it never billed, and a registered one got a forecast ~13% above the basis its Labor Forecast

2. **The day of the run is never a sample.** The orders read had a lower bound and no upper one,

3. **The delete of the previous run checked nothing.** Both `scopedDelete` calls were bare awaits.

4. **`.eq('source','manual')` dropped NULL-source rows.** `persistSalesDay.js` treats NULL as

6. **`sample_count` and `pos_sample_count` are stored** on the covers-level row (the migration).

### S693 — 2026-09-07 — The Labor Forecast learns how many hours the business needs, from its own history

**No migration, no Edge Function deploy.** `CACHE_NAME` and `APP_VERSION` both bumped to
`crest-v228`. Follows S692 in the same session: Aashish asked what a labour forecast means, then
whether it should not also compute how many staff or man-hours the business requires. It should.
Until now the only labour standard in the product was `settings.covers_per_staff_target` — one
hand-typed number, default 20, that nothing ever learned, that needed covers (so an outlet without
POS got no staffing guidance at all), and that was one figure for a whole outlet.

**`src/modules/hr/roster/laborStandard.js` is the new pure module** (no Supabase import, so it is
testable — `demandForecastData.js` is not, which is why its three pure exports never got a test
file). It derives **sales per labour hour** from a trailing 120-day window and answers
`requiredHoursFor(revenue, std, weekday, department)` and `requiredStaffFor(hours, typicalShift)`.
Four arithmetic choices are load-bearing:

- **A ratio of totals, never a mean of per-day ratios** — the rule this repo already applies to
  group figures, and here it correctly weights busy days, which is where a staffing error costs
  most. A test pins the two apart on a skewed fixture.
- **Linear through the origin, no fixed-crew intercept.** Real F&B has an opening crew, but the
  per-weekday split absorbs most of what an intercept would do (a quiet Monday is calibrated
  against its own quiet Mondays, which carry their own fixed crew), and a two-parameter regression
  produces a number nobody can check by hand. Stated in the module header so the next reader knows
  it was a choice.
- **Typical shift hours is `Σ hours / Σ heads` from the same window**, never `STANDARD_HOURS_PER_DAY`
  — that is the statutory day, a payroll constant, not a rostering fact about this outlet.
- **A pooled MAD trim at 3×**, reported on screen. One rule catches a festival, a closure, a
  part-entered attendance sheet and a bulk-sales artefact. Below 5 samples nothing is trimmed: a
  trim you cannot justify is a guess. Pooled rather than per weekday, because a weekday bucket holds
  at most ~17 days in this window and a MAD on that is noise.

**Two bases, and the app measures the gap between them rather than assuming it.** Aashish said
Attendance is filled in one batch at month end, and that rostered hours run within 15–20% of real
attendance. So attendance days are the strong basis and roster days the weaker one — without the
latter the model would be blind to the entire current month. For days carrying **both**,
`measureRosterBias` computes `Σ attendanceHours / Σ rosteredHours` and roster-only samples are
scaled by it; the figure is printed on the tab ("adjusted by the 17% gap measured over 63 days that
had both"), which is a management number the product did not offer before. It applies only past 10
overlap days, and **never assumes a direction** — overtime can push the ratio above 1. Below the
floor the roster hours train unadjusted and the tab says so.

**The severe trap, caught in design review rather than in production: departed employees.**
`computeActualLabor` skips any attendance row whose employee is not in the list it was handed, and
Roster loads `status IN ('active','probation')`. Reusing it for a four-month window would have
discarded every hour worked by anyone who has since left — window hours low → sales per labour hour
high → **required hours LOW**, i.e. the model telling the owner to roster fewer people than his own
history says he needed, in the direction that costs service. `tallyWindowAttendance` filters by
nothing and the window's employee read carries no status filter. This is S633's rule — a history
outlives the people in it — arriving on a second surface.

**On screen, no new column.** The table is already eight wide with a wrap risk, so everything rides
the small-print second line the tab already uses: `need 31h` under Hours (on a past day, beside
`plan 27h`, computed from what the day actually earned — the retrospective verdict), and the basis
under Recommended Staff (`from 6 Tuesdays`, `from all 24 days`, or `covers ÷ 20`). The basis rides
per row on purpose: a Tuesday with six samples and a Friday falling back to all-days are different
bases in one view and a single footnote could not say so. The footer carries the period requirement.
**Recommended Staff and Status now render on `hasPos || standard.ok`** — the standard is
revenue-based, so an outlet with no till gets a staffing figure for the first time.

**Honesty rules, each rendering a dash with a reason rather than a number:** under 20 qualifying
days there is no standard at all (never a thin figure from six days); a weekday under 4 samples
falls back to all-days and **says so**; a failed window read shows `ActionError` and says it is a
failed read, **not** a lack of history; a coming day with no forecast revenue gets no requirement,
never an average substituted to keep the cell full; a department with no window hours gets nothing,
never the outlet's figure scaled by a guess; unknown shift length shows required hours and dashes
the head count. A bulk `bs_day = 0` sales month is barred from training entirely — its revenue is
understated with no day to attach it to, which would deflate the standard and **inflate** required
hours everywhere. `revenue_estimated` is now selected from `demand_forecast_daily` (written by
`runForecast` since S399, never read here) and marks a requirement built on an estimated revenue.

**The settings write was silently a no-op.** `covers_per_staff_target` was read by a second
`settings` query duplicating SettingsContext's own `select('*')` — both queries dropped their error,
so a failed `clients` read silently printed a schedule with no letterhead — and written by a bare
`.update().eq('client_id')` with no existing-row guard and no error check, so for a client with no
settings row it never saved at all. Both now go through context and `saveSettings`; the input is
controlled off a draft so a rejected save reverts rather than showing a number the database refused.
The learned covers-per-shift figure sits **beside** the target as a read-only hint and never
overwrites it: the target is the service standard the owner wants, the learned figure is what the
outlet did, and collapsing the two destroys the ability to say "we are understaffing against our own
standard."

**Reads.** A separate loader from `loadActuals` — different key (the client, not the visible range),
different lifetime (once, not per month arrow), different employee filter, anchored on today rather
than the viewed month — with its own `useLatestRequest` keyed on `clientId` so an admin switching
clients with the tab open cannot race. `hr_attendance`, `hr_roster`, `sales_entries` and `recipes`
are all paged (`hr_roster` is `hr_attendance`'s twin at ~34 staff/month and had been unpaged in
`computeLaborAnalyticsSection` too). The derived standard — not the ~15k raw rows — is cached in
`sessionDataCache`, which the tab passes both adoption tests for: it batch-saves nothing from
on-screen state, and the standard IS the content of the new cells, so caching genuinely shortens the
revisit. **No table and no migration**: a derived figure that can always be recomputed does not earn

`20260907120000`.

### S675 — 2026-09-04 — every tenant Owner was locked out of the product by a filter that matched nothing

**Service worker `crest-v187` → `crest-v188`. No migration, no Edge Function deploy.**
**This was a live, total lockout of every client Owner, and accepting could not clear it.**

`AuthContext.js` read the acceptance ledger with
`.in('doc_type', docsRequiringReacceptance())` — but that helper returns document **objects**.
PostgREST stringifies whatever it is handed, so the filter was on the literal text
`[object Object]`, matched no rows, and returned `{ data: [], error: null }`. That is a *successful*
read stating the client has accepted nothing. The memo therefore held every Owner at the blocking
gate, and the acceptance they submitted was written correctly and then never seen by the re-read.

**Both of the safeguards built for exactly this screen were bypassed, and for the same reason.** The
read fails OPEN on error — but `legalErr` was null, so that branch was never reached. And S672 had
already been bitten by the objects-versus-strings distinction in the *other* direction, documented
at length in `legal-documents.md` with the same predicted symptom ("held at the blocking screen
permanently, with no way out except Sign Out"). `reacceptGuard.test.js` was written to catch it and
tests the helper, which was correct both times. **The fault was at the call site, which passed the
wrong one of two correct functions — so a test of the helper passes happily while the product is
shut.**

The fix is structural rather than a corrected argument: `reacceptDocTypes()` now exists beside
`docsRequiringReacceptance()`, and the rule is that anything comparing against the `doc_type`
COLUMN calls the former while anything reading a version or hash calls the latter — neither caller
does the `.map` itself. Two new tests read the **source** and fail if any `.in('doc_type', …)` in
`src/` is given anything but a types-shaped value; both were verified against the exact string that
shipped rather than assumed to work.

Found by shipping S674's recovery message first, which is the only reason the state was
diagnosable: the gate said "your acceptance was recorded but this screen did not refresh", which
distinguished a failed write from a failed read. 52 suites / 614 tests.
