# Login and sign-up pages

Split out of `.claude/rules/auth-and-pins.md` word for word (S770 context pass, 2026-09-17), so Edge Functions, POS devices and staff screens stop loading page-layout detail for three screens. Credential and PIN mechanics stay in `auth-and-pins.md`.

### Login/auth pages: seven things that were wrong once and are easy to reintroduce

1. **A generic error message is right for credentials and wrong for everything else.** `signInErrorMessage()` in `Login.js` keeps one indistinguishable string across every credential outcome (wrong password, no such account, disabled) — that's the OWASP anti-enumeration control and must not be softened into "no account found". But it separates out 429s, 5xx and the 15s `makeAuthTimeoutFetch` abort, because someone who has been rate-limited being told their password is wrong just retries harder. New auth failure paths need the same split.
2. **`@media (pointer: coarse)`, not a width breakpoint, for touch sizing.** `Login.css` bumps inputs to 16px and buttons to a 48px minimum there. 16px is the threshold below which iOS Safari zooms the viewport on focus and never zooms back — the trigger is the input method, not the screen size, and scoping it by width would have disturbed the desktop hero layout for no reason.

    **These values are the login page's own, not the product's floor — and for a long time they were mistaken for it.** Until 2026-08-23/S603 the 16px rule existed *here and nowhere else*, so every field in the authenticated app stayed 13px on a tablet while DESIGN.md described 16px as the product's strategy. `Layout.css`'s `pointer: coarse` block now carries an element-level `font-size: 16px !important` for `input`/`select`/`textarea` plus `min-height: 44px` for `button:not([class])`. Login keeps its own richer tier (48px buttons, 13px/14px padding) because this page is laid out to a measured viewport budget; don't delete it in favour of the global floor, and don't assume a rule living in `Login.css` covers anything but `/login` and `/reset-password`.
3. **`.login-page` is its own scrollport, and it has to be (S604).** `index.css`'s app-wide `html, body { overflow-x: hidden }` makes `body` a scroll container sized to its content, so `.login-nav`'s `position: sticky` had a scrollport that never scrolled — measured on the built page at 390×780, its viewport top went `0 → −250 → −600`, i.e. it had never stuck since it was written. `.login-page` now takes `height: 100dvh; overflow-y: auto` instead of `min-height: 100vh`; the min-height had to go, because 100vh ≥ 100dvh pushes the root taller than its own scrollport and hands the scroll back to the body. **This does not change the S553/S560 fit budget** — the content is the same height and simply overflows a container instead of the body — but it does change how you MEASURE it: `document.documentElement.scrollHeight` vs `window.innerHeight` is no longer the fit test, since the document no longer scrolls. Measure `.login-page`'s own `scrollHeight` vs its `clientHeight`. Relaxing the body rule instead was measured and rejected: `html`-only loses the horizontal guard entirely (1610px of overflow on a probe page).

4. **An inline `<p className="login-error">` announces nothing.** Errors need `role="alert"` and confirmation panels `role="status"`; the login page shipped for a long time with zero live regions, so a screen-reader user got no indication that a submit had failed or that a reset email had been sent. Verify with a live DOM query (`[role=alert],[role=status],[aria-live]`), not by reading the JSX.
5. **The whole page must fit one screen, and that is a measured claim (S553).** `/login` is the one screen where every element competes for the same viewport, so it is laid out to fit without scrolling from ~830px of viewport height up: section paddings are `clamp(floor, Nvh, ceiling)`, and an `@media (min-width: 981px) and (max-height: 1000px)` tier drops them to their floors across the whole common desktop band. Measured with Playwright against the real `build/` (`playwright-core` plus the Chromium already on this machine — serve `build/` over a throwaway `http.createServer` and read `document.documentElement.scrollHeight` vs `window.innerHeight`): 91px of slack at 1920x950, 61px at 1920x912, 23px at 1536x864; 1366x768 scrolls by 60px and is meant to. **Anything added to this page costs one of those margins** — re-measure rather than assuming.

    **Those margins are now spent, and the page no longer fully meets the budget.** T12's pitch rewrite (three modules instead of one) added ~130px of column height, so **the pitch column, not the sign-in card, is what drives the hero's height** — the reverse of what this rule said for a year, and the reason the sentence is worth re-reading before adding anything here. Re-measured against the real build on 2026-09-01: 1920x950 fits with **0px** to spare, 1920x912 scrolls 21px, 1536x864 scrolls 61px, 1366x768 scrolls 146px, 1366x613 fits with 0px. The cost is bounded and was checked — the sign-in card stays above the fold at every one of those sizes, so what falls below is the trial band, which the header CTA anchor-links to anyway. A 0px result is not slack: S553's own warning applies, that a fit landing exactly on the line is not a fit. The visible POS/IMS/HR module headings that pitch briefly carried were cut for this reason alone (~93px), so grouping is not available as a saving a second time.

    **S560 added a second, more aggressive tier for genuinely short windows.** A real user's browser measured 1366x613 (a non-maximized window with heavy chrome, not a phone) and overflowed by 201px even under the S553 tier — that tier's own floor assumes ~830px and up, and 613px is well below it. `@media (min-width: 981px) and (max-height: 700px)` steps nav height, section padding, headings, form-field sizing and button heights down further on the shared `.login-field`/`.login-btn`/`.login-staff-btn` rules, mirroring the `pointer: coarse` tier's approach but shrinking instead of growing. Verified with Playwright at 1366x613: 0px overflow when it shipped, and still 0px after T12 — but 0px is now the whole margin rather than a comfortable one. Deliberately scoped narrow (`max-height: 700px`) rather than widened to swallow the 768px case — 1366x768 scrolling by 60px is S553's own documented, intentional tradeoff, not a bug this fix should silently reopen.
6. **Two traps in any contrast-measuring script written against this codebase**, both of which produced phantom AA failures during S553 and would recur. A **semi-transparent layer must be composited over its ancestors** — the codebase's standard tint pattern is `color-mix(… 10%, transparent)`, and reading that as an opaque fill reports a near-white background as the full-strength accent. And **Chrome serialises a `color-mix()` result as `color(srgb 0.98 0.92 0.89 / 0.1)`, components in 0..1, not 0..255** — a parser that assumes 8-bit turns a pale tint into near-black and fails everything using it. With both handled, every text colour on `/login` measures ≥5.09:1 on Rosé Dawn.
7. **`/login` is a page, not a card (S553).** It was a single 1020px card centred in an empty viewport — which is also why everything inside it was sized small enough to fit that box and only read comfortably at 125% browser zoom. It now carries a sticky header, a hero (pitch left, sign-in card right), a slim signup band and a footer, structurally mirroring `Pricing.js` so the two public surfaces read as one site. **The band held the whole trial form until S689 moved it to its own `/signup` route** — the page was carrying two jobs with opposite consequences, one signs you in and the other creates a live client record, and it could not fit its own one-screen budget while doing both. What is left in that slot is the invitation: a 2px rule, the question, the terms in one line, and an outlined CTA. The sign-in card is the only remaining box, which is correct — a form is a card; a page is not. Consequences for anyone editing it: the page's `h1` is the hero headline, so the card's headings are `h2`; the header's `Start free trial →` navigates to `/signup` (S689). It was an `<a href="#start-trial">` in-page jump whose band needed `scroll-margin-top` to clear the sticky header; both the anchor and that rule are gone with the band; and **`Pricing.js`'s 44px hero is an inline style in JS, which the impeccable hook cannot see** — it is not a licence to go off DESIGN.md's closed type ramp in CSS, where the same value gets flagged.
